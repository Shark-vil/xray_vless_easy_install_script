Repotting the fig before spring

March 9

The little fig spent winter by the north window and dropped every leaf, which is exactly what it is supposed to do. This week I slid it out of the pot to check the roots: circling a bit, but healthy and pale. I teased the outer inch loose, trimmed two thick roots, and moved it up one pot size with a gritty mix.

Note for next year: do this two weeks earlier. The buds were already swelling and a couple snapped off while I worked.

A cheap soil-moisture meter that actually lasts

February 22

The resistive probes you find for a dollar corrode within a season because they run a DC current straight through wet soil. The capacitive ones avoid that by measuring at a fixed frequency instead. I potted the electronics in a blob of hot glue, left only the sensing pad exposed, and it has read steadily for months.

Calibration was two points: bone-dry pot, then the same pot an hour after a full soak. Everything useful happens between those numbers.

The long way around the reservoir

February 3

Fourteen kilometres, mostly frozen gravel. I have walked the short loop a hundred times but never the full perimeter. The far side has an old pumping station, brick, roof half gone, with a hand-lettered sign that just says NO ENTRY — 1987. Someone has kept the padlock oiled anyway.
